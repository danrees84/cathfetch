## Cycle A

### Easter sunday

Reading set: `easter_sunday:A:vigil` · Mass form: **vigil**

**Romans 6:4** — Douay–Rheims (1899)

> For we are buried together with him by baptism into death; that as Christ is risen from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we also may walk in newness of life.

Paul describes baptism as participation in Christ's death and resurrection. Burial with Christ marks a decisive break with sin's dominion, ordered towards a new way of living. The resurrection is consequently more than reassurance about the future: it gives the baptised a present vocation. The Easter Vigil proclaims this passage as the Church celebrates baptism and renews its promises.

**Selection rationale:** The epistle binds the Vigil's salvation history to its baptismal celebration.

### Holy week · 5

Reading set: `temporal:holy_week:5:any:passion` · Mass form: **passion**

**Hebrews 4:15** — Douay–Rheims (1899)

> For we have not a high priest, who can not have compassion on our infirmities: but one tempted in all things like as we are, without sin.

Hebrews grounds confidence before God in the character of our high priest. Christ's sinlessness does not place him beyond compassion: he has entered human trial without yielding to sin. His solidarity with our weakness therefore opens access to mercy, rather than confirming a distance we cannot cross. Good Friday contemplates this priestly compassion in the crucified Lord.
