Reading set: `temporal:ordinary:34:2:I:day` · Mass form: **day**

**Luke 21:8** — Douay–Rheims (1899)

> Who said: Take heed you be not seduced; for many will come in my name, saying, I am he; and the time is at hand: go ye not therefore after them.

Jesus' warning begins by forbidding pursuit of claimants who announce that the decisive time has arrived. The discourse is meant to form steadfastness, not a market for alarm or calculation. Attention to Christ's own teaching protects the community from being led by the excitement of confident predictions. The closing weekday sequence prepares for fulfilment through fidelity rather than sensational expectation.

### Wednesday, week 34 of Ordinary Time

Reading set: `temporal:ordinary:34:3:I:day` · Mass form: **day**

**Luke 21:19** — Douay–Rheims (1899)

> In your patience you shall possess your souls.

Jesus speaks of endurance within an account of betrayal, persecution and testimony. Patience is not passive indifference to harm, but steadfast belonging to him through conditions that threaten to undo it. The promise locates the disciple's true life beyond the immediate success of hostile powers. The Gospel gives the Church language for hope without concealing the cost of witness.

### Thursday, week 34 of Ordinary Time

Reading set: `temporal:ordinary:34:4:I:day` · Mass form: **day**

**Luke 21:28** — Douay–Rheims (1899)

> But when these things begin to come to pass, look up, and lift up your heads, because your redemption is at hand.

Jesus directs those facing upheaval to lift their heads in hope of redemption. The imagery does not invite delight in disaster or certainty about its date. Its purpose is to prevent fear from becoming the final horizon for people who await the saving coming of the Son of Man. The year's closing readings turn expectation towards deliverance rather than despair.

### Saturday, week 34 of Ordinary Time

Reading set: `temporal:ordinary:34:6:I:day` · Mass form: **day**

**Luke 21:36** — Douay–Rheims (1899)

> Watch ye, therefore, praying at all times, that you may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that are to come, and to stand before the Son of man.

Jesus joins watchfulness to continual prayer at the end of his discourse about coming trials. Readiness is not anxious scrutiny of every event, but a life kept open before God.
