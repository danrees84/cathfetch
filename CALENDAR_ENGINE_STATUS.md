# Cathfetch calendar engine status

As of 14 October 2026, the engine implements the contemporary General Roman Calendar and the supplied European, English and Welsh overlays. Every date in 2024–2030 resolves to an observed day in all four calendars. Corpus Christi also resolves on 24 June 2038, with the Nativity of John the Baptist provisionally anticipated to 23 June; only the transfer date awaits authoritative confirmation. This is a contemporary calendar snapshot, not a historical or diocesan ordo.

## Resolution and coverage

`resolveLiturgicalDay(date, celebrations, calendarName)` validates the input, constructs the fixed observance schedule, gathers movable candidates, and selects against one temporal candidate. Rank and precedence remain distinct. General and proper classifications cover all thirteen levels, including proper feasts in England and Wales and All Souls without an ordinary rank.

Lent, Holy Week, the Triduum, both octaves, Easter weekdays and Sundays, Advent, Christmas and Ordinary Time all have explicit temporal designations. Movable candidates include Holy Family, Baptism, Trinity, Corpus Christi, Sacred Heart, Christ the King, Ascension, Pentecost, Easter, Mother of the Church, Immaculate Heart and the national Eternal High Priest feast. Ordinary Time Saturdays retain the permitted Marian memorial as an alternative.

`namesTemporalBaseline` supplies the identity of an existing temporal candidate. It must match that candidate's precedence and must be unique. It cannot promote a celebration to a stronger class. A general-calendar solemnity of the Lord prevails over a coinciding general-calendar solemnity of a saint at the same numerical class 3, unless the principal-patron exception requires a local disposition. Unspecified equal memorials or feasts are rejected with a diagnostic. Explicit occurrence dispositions govern Mother of the Church, Immaculate Heart and the Eternal High Priest feast when it meets Augustine of Canterbury.

The default on optional memorial days is the temporal weekday. `alternatives` retains the choices in stable order. On privileged weekdays, eligible alternatives have `restrictedCommemoration = true`; obligatory memorials in Lent are reduced accordingly. Alternatives are omitted when a higher celebration excludes them. This API does not select Mass or Office texts.

`LiturgicalDay` exposes the selected identity, rank, precedence, season, colour, alternatives and transfer provenance. `scheduledFrom` records a national annual scheduling difference from the nominal date. `transferredFrom` records displacement by an impediment. `provisionalTransfer` and `transferNote` qualify an unconfirmed transfer date. On the original day, `displacedObservances` preserves each transferred solemnity and its destination. Permanent national date reassignments remain changes to the inherited fixed facts.

The terminal gives the season a dot in its normal seasonal colour and gives each named celebration a separate dot in its resolved observance colour. Temporal days also receive an observance dot when their colour differs from the season, such as Palm Sunday and Good Friday. Ordinary weekdays retain just the season dot. The Triduum has no single seasonal colour, so its season line has no dot. Violet maps to the terminal magenta/purple ANSI colour rather than blue. Provisional transfers are labelled explicitly. It preserves the existing date, calendar and cross options and reports invalid inputs and unresolved occurrences on stderr. The engine has no terminal or Qt dependency.

Terminal rendering explicitly wraps words into rows, with continuation indentation after colour bullets. At 55 columns or wider, the original cross stays on the left with the information column at cell 23; below 55 columns, the full cross is centred above the information. Without `--cross`, text uses the full available width. Below the artwork's intrinsic eleven-cell width, a compact cross is used. Width detection queries stdout with Linux `TIOCGWINSZ`, then uses a valid `COLUMNS` value, then defaults to 80 columns for redirected output. ANSI colour and hyperlink escapes occupy no cells; UTF-8 is decoded safely and character widths follow libc `wcwidth` in a thread-local UTF-8 character locale. Combining marks and common joined emoji remain together during hard word breaks. Ambiguous glyph widths can vary with terminal font settings.

## Verified rules and sources

The [Universal Norms](https://www.liturgyoffice.org.uk/Calendar/Info/GNLY.pdf), especially paragraphs 5, 14–16, 19, 22–39 and 60, govern seasons, restricted memorials, ordinary Sunday impediments and eligible transfer destinations. The audit reviewed dates, ranks, identities, Lord-feast classifications and inheritance against the [baseline calendar](https://www.liturgyoffice.org.uk/Calendar/Info/General-Roman-Calendar.pdf) and the recorded Holy See amendments. Those amendments include Magdalene, Martha/Mary/Lazarus, the three Doctors added in 2021, Paul VI, Faustina, Loreto, John XXIII, John Paul II, Teresa of Calcutta and Newman. Irenaeus's designation now includes Doctor of the Church, with its [2022 decree](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/bollettino/pubblico/2022/01/21/220121b.html) recorded in the entry.

The five Welsh entries previously inferred as obligatory from older descriptive pages are optional in this snapshot: Asaph, John Jones, Philip Evans and John Lloyd, David Lewis, and John Roberts. The national summary and [2026 published calendar](https://www.liturgyoffice.org.uk/Calendar/2026/Calendar-2026.pdf) govern their classification. Published annual calendars can contain inconsistent labels; the explicit Holy See memorial collision dispositions take precedence over a conflicting annual label.

## Validation and use

Run from the repository root:

```sh
make -j4 all test
./build/cathfetch --date=02032026 --calendar=wales --cross
./build/calendar_year 2026 england > /tmp/cathfetch-2026-england.tsv
```

The existing nlohmann JSON dependency remains required. No new runtime library was added. `calendar_year` produces one TSV record per date, including alternatives, scheduling, transfers and diagnostics.

The suite contains the six original test executables, four additional C++ test executables, and CLI integration checks. The comprehensive test covers 44 complete years in each calendar: 1818, 1900, 1943 and every year from 2000 through 2040. Its 64,284 dates are each resolved in both forward and reversed definition order. Checks include unique annual identities, alternative ordering, colours, week ranges, transfer destinations, duplicate definitions, invalid inputs and transactional overlay failure. Published dates and ordo examples provide independent reference cases. Every tested date now has a resolved observance, including the 2038 and projected 1943 occurrences. The occurrence regression also checks subject-based priority with arbitrary IDs, equal numerical classes, preserved transfers, candidate order, and the 2022 principal-patron exception. Validation passed with `make -j4 all test`: all ten C++ test executables and the CLI checks pass, with no compiler warnings. The comprehensive test passes 450,238 checks over all 64,284 dates, each resolved in both candidate orders. The focused solemnity occurrence regression also passes AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviourSanitizer, with LeakSanitizer disabled for the tracing environment. Generated 2038 TSV diagnostics for `grc` and `europe` each contain 365 resolved records and explicitly label the provisional June 23 transfer.

Rendering regression tests cover 120, 100, 80, 60 and 40 columns with and without the cross, plus the stacking boundary and extremely narrow widths. Pseudo-terminal integration checks exercise actual ioctl sizing against conflicting `COLUMNS`, and redirected-output checks exercise explicit widths and the 80-column fallback. They verify intact artwork, complete names, hanging indentation, colour preservation and cell-width bounds. The rendering test and changed terminal code also pass AddressSanitizer and UndefinedBehaviourSanitizer; LeakSanitizer is disabled for this execution environment.

## Limits and remaining authority questions

- The computational input range is Gregorian 1583–4099. All years use the current snapshot of fixed facts and national rules. Earlier dates do not reconstruct the calendar then in force; future dates cannot anticipate decrees. The primary contemporary validation range is 2024–2030.
- On 24 June 2038, Thursday Corpus Christi coincides with the Nativity of John the Baptist in `grc` and `europe`, both class 3. Corpus Christi takes priority as a solemnity of the Lord; John retains solemnity rank and class 3 and transfers rather than being omitted. By the user's chosen policy and analogy with the 2022 disposition, the engine provisionally anticipates John to 23 June. The preceding-day choice is not an official determination for 2038. June 25 retains its ordinary calendar. The modern-rule projection of 1943 follows the same provisional policy and is not the historical calendar of 1943.
- The [2022 CDW disposition as reported by the diocesan liturgy office](https://liturgia.diocesiudine.it/2022/06/06/precisazione-sulle-solennita-del-sacratissimo-cuore-di-gesu-e-della-nativita-di-san-giovanni-battista/) supports the Lord-versus-saint priority, while the [Bishops' Conference notes](https://www.liturgyoffice.org.uk/Calendar/2022/Ordo-2022.pdf) explicitly record the patronal reversal: John remains on June 24 and Sacred Heart anticipates to June 23. A definition with `principal_patron = true` honours that exception, including when John's rank comes from a proper calendar. The engine does not extrapolate that reversal into permission to move Corpus Christi in an unspecified local calendar; such a 2038 local occurrence remains explicitly pending its own disposition. None of the four supplied calendars designates John as principal patron.
- The anticipation policy searches backwards if the preferred preceding day is impeded. General transfers still use the nearest eligible day and prefer the following day in an otherwise unspecified tie; that preference is a deterministic software policy, not a tie-break prescribed by paragraph 60. Equal solemnities without a recognised occurrence rule remain diagnostic cases.
- Date-only season labels treat Holy Thursday as Lent, Friday and Saturday as Triduum, and Easter Sunday as Easter Time with Triduum precedence. Holy Thursday and Holy Saturday have no single resolved colour because their evening celebrations differ. There is no Vespers, vigil, time-of-day or concurrence calculation.
- Violet is the deterministic All Souls colour; permitted alternatives such as black, rose on the appropriate Sundays and festive vestments are not separately modelled.
- Diocesan, parish, church dedication, religious-order and other local propers are absent. `europe` adds regional patrons and uses base scheduling; it does not represent every European nation's calendar. Arbitrary local calendar extensions can require additional authoritative dispositions.
- Holy-day obligations, readings and devotional or pastoral substitutions are outside this engine. Data consists of independently structured facts and source links, without copied liturgical texts or biographies.

No user design decision is needed for the implemented contemporary calendars. The provisional 23 June transfer date needs authoritative confirmation; Corpus Christi priority is resolved independently of that uncertainty.
